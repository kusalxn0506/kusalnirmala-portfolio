The Problem

Boats don't carry signal with them

Fishery boats spend most of a trip well past the point where a mobile network reaches — which means the tracking systems most vessels rely on simply stop working once they're out far enough to actually need them. That gap was the brief: build something that keeps reporting a boat's position without depending on cellular infrastructure at all.

The Build

LoRa, and a PCB we etched ourselves

We built around LoRa — a radio protocol designed for exactly this trade-off: long range and low power, at the cost of bandwidth, which is fine when all you're sending is a position, not video. A transmitter unit on the boat streams location back to a base-station receiver on shore, over a link that doesn't care whether a cell tower is anywhere nearby. The board carrying it was ours — designed, laid out, and assembled by the team rather than bought off the shelf.

Hardware Stack

Inside the Controller

The tracking device is built around the LoLin NodeMCU V3, an ESP8266-based WiFi microcontroller that orchestrates all sensor data, LoRa radio communication, and GPS positioning.

Microcontroller
ESP8266 — 80 MHz Tensilica L106
4 MB Flash, 80 KB RAM
802.11 b/g/n WiFi
Radio Module
LoRa SX1278 — 433 MHz
Long-range sub-GHz transceiver
Up to 10 km line-of-sight
Positioning
GPS NEO-6M
UART interface at 9600 baud
NMEA sentence parsing
Power
5V USB / 3.3V LDO
Regulated supply for all modules
Battery-ready design
Competition & Result

2nd Runner-up, University Category

The build was entered into the SLIOT tournament, hosted at the University of Moratuwa, where it placed 2nd runner-up in the university category. Coverage of the event and the product came from the University of Moratuwa and SLTMobitel.
